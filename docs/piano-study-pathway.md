# Piano study pathway

Research checked October 8, 2026 against Piano Marvel's own public explanations:

- [Dashboard](https://pianomarvel.com/en/feature/dashboard): separate Library, Method, Technique and Sight Reading areas; 600 exercises in each of Method and Technique.
- [Teacher certification](https://pianomarvel.com/en/courses/teacher-certification-course): reading, technique, scales/arpeggios and breaking a passage into smaller practice groups are complementary activities.
- [Preparation for Recitals](https://pianomarvel.com/en/article/preparation-for-recitals), Evelyn Billberg: practice the key's scales, chords and arpeggios in preparation for a piece, and develop recovery through uninterrupted performances.
- [Sight-reading assessment](https://pianomarvel.com/en/article/the-ultimate-sight-reading-test), Heidi Garner: advance preview, counted entry, continuous performance and fresh material. Their 90 difficulty buckets and calibrated SASR ranking belong to their assessment; our tasks are independently authored, approximate exercises, without standardized rankings.

Our seven repertoire levels now have five original method/technique units each. They cover pulse and intervals, articulation and positions, major/minor triads and inversions, arpeggios and dominant sevenths, harmonic minor and voice leading, syncopation/counterpoint/secondary dominants, and voicing/structure/performance. Each unit names a complete playable piece and a self-check. Advanced repertoire does not imply advanced sight reading, so reading tasks have a separate selector.

The harmony laboratory spells all twelve major and twelve natural-minor scales diatonically (including necessary double flats), demonstrates major/minor thirds, relative versus parallel keys, and I–IV–V7 / i–iv–V7 harmony. In minor it explicitly explains the raised leading tone of the major dominant. Samples use the existing local acoustic grand piano. The accompanying 24 original two-hand eight-bar studies connect melodic fragments with cadences. They are complete studies, not fragments of famous pieces. They do not add fingering numbers. Each key study carries a `studyLevel` from the usual teaching order of keys: C, G and F major with A, D and E minor at level 2; D and B♭ major with B and G minor at 3; A and E♭ major with F♯ and C minor at 4; E and A♭ major with C♯ minor (kept under its existing id `key-des-minor`) and F minor at 5; the remaining six keys at 6.

## Level 0 · First keys

Twenty complete original pieces (`first-01` … `first-20`, kind `first`, `studyLevel` 0) give an absolute beginner one new demand at a time: right hand alone in C position, steps and repeated notes (1–3, whole notes from 2); left hand alone in C position in the bass (4–5); a melody passed between the hands with skips and rests, never both at once (6–8); hands together over a held left-hand note, then left-hand half notes moving by step and fifth (9–11); G position with the first F♯ (12–13); 3/4 with dotted half notes (14–15); middle-C position, both thumbs sharing middle C (16); F position with the first B♭ (17); fourths and fifths with legato slurs and staccato (18); a melody over a I–V accompaniment in open fifths (19); and first eighth-note pairs in a small graduation march (20). Each is 8–12 bars, ends on the tonic, prints a tempo word and metronome mark (♩ = 76–96, also the MIDI tempo) and is engraved larger, one four-bar phrase per line. A resting hand shows whole-bar rests; pieces 1–3 and 12 are right hand alone and 4–5 left hand alone, so their practice data has one hand. The manifest gives each piece `studyOrder`, `focus`, `tempo` and `timeSignature`. No fingering numbers are printed.

## First-reading miniatures

Seventy complete original eight-bar miniatures, ten at each of seven rough reading levels. The original fourteen (`reading-L-1`, `reading-L-2`) keep their ids and music, so learners' seen state still refers to them. The other 56 (`reading-L-3` … `reading-L-10`) come from a seeded, constrained melodic generator in `scripts/build-piano-studies.py`: its own small random generator (mulberry32 seeded from the id) makes every build write the same music. Per level it fixes keys, meters, rhythm cells, melodic range, largest leap and a left-hand pattern:

| Level | Keys | Meters | Rhythm | Left hand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C | 4/4 | quarters, halves, whole notes; steps and skips in C position | whole notes |
| 2 | C, G, F | 4/4, 3/4 | adds dotted halves; five-finger positions | moving half notes in position |
| 3 | G, F, E minor, D minor | 4/4, 3/4, 2/4 | eighth notes | broken chords |
| 4 | D, B♭, B minor, G minor | 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 | dotted quarter + eighth | bass note and chord |
| 5 | A, E♭, F♯ minor, C minor | 4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 2/4 | sixteenths; ledger lines | Alberti bass |
| 6 | up to three sharps or flats | 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 | two-note right-hand textures, chromatic lower neighbours | chords or Alberti |
| 7 | E, A♭, C♯ minor, F minor | 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 | triplets, wider leaps (to the octave) | arpeggios |

Each piece is a four-bar antecedent ending on a chord tone of the dominant and a four-bar consequent ending on the tonic, approached by step, over a progression from a small set (I–IV–V, with vi and ii from level 3; harmonic minor, so the dominant has its raised leading tone). Bars 5–6 often restate bars 1–2. Strong beats and long notes are chord tones; other notes are passing or neighbour tones moved to and left by step. Augmented and diminished leaps, sevenths and leaps beyond the octave are excluded, a leap turns back, sixteenths move by step, repeated long notes and a-b-a-b wobbles are refused, the right hand stays above the left, and parallel octaves or fifths between the outer voices onto a strong beat are rejected. Among a dozen valid melodies the build keeps the one with the smoothest line, a single high point, a compact range near the middle of the level's register and the fewest remaining parallels.

Opening a score marks it seen, including a selection through the main player. The picker excludes seen material and reports exhaustion honestly; it cannot claim unlimited fresh reading. Open the score, scan without rehearsal, connect MIDI, and use the moving-sheet assessment for one uninterrupted attempt. Reopening a known piece is labeled rehearsal. Pitch/timing results do not assess musical phrasing or held-note duration; level advancement remains a self-check.

## Repertoire additions

Two additional complete public-domain Burgmüller works fill compound-meter and paired-note gaps: [La Pastorale, Op.100 No.3](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=218) and [La Petite Réunion, Op.100 No.4](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=219), typeset by Bas Wassink. Original PDFs/sources remain intact; practice versions unfold repeats and retain printed fingerings. No Piano Marvel lesson, arrangement or SASR test item is copied.

Thirteen famous complete works were added from Mutopia (`scripts/piano-famous-sources.json`, manifest `site/piano-famous.js`): Chopin's Fantaisie-Impromptu, Minute Waltz and Raindrop Prelude; Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata (first movement); Mozart's Rondo alla Turca; Rachmaninoff's Prelude in C♯ minor; Debussy's Arabesque No. 1; Joplin's Maple Leaf Rag; Grieg's own piano version of In the Hall of the Mountain King; Satie's Gnossienne No. 1; Schubert's Impromptu D. 899 No. 3; Liszt's Consolation No. 3; and Brahms's Waltz Op. 39 No. 15. Chopin's Ballade No. 1 was not taken from Mutopia because that edition's printed and MIDI music differ deliberately; it comes from the MusicXML set below. Each score README lists any practice-copy correction.

`python3 scripts/prepare-mutopia-score.py WORK scripts/piano-famous-sources.json` downloads the sources, flattens multi-file uploads, converts to LilyPond 2.24, unfolds repeats, applies the documented per-score fixups and compiles tagged SVG and MIDI. Then run `read-piano-library-midi.cjs` and `build-piano-library.py WORK scripts/piano-famous-sources.json site/piano-famous.js`, and `scripts/add-famous-curriculum.py`. Scores with more than two MIDI tracks take every hand from the tagged engraving; the build fails if any note is left without a hand.

Eleven more famous works come from MuseScore transcriptions that their authors released under CC0 (one under the Public Domain Mark), found through the PDMX dataset (https://zenodo.org/records/15571083) and re-checked on each MuseScore page: Liszt's La Campanella and Liebestraum No. 3; Chopin's Ballade No. 1, Heroic Polonaise, Études Op. 10 No. 3 "Tristesse", Op. 10 No. 4 and Op. 25 No. 11 "Winter Wind", the Nocturnes in C♯ minor (B. 49), B♭ minor (Op. 9 No. 1) and C minor (Op. 48 No. 1), and the Waltz in C♯ minor, Op. 64 No. 2 (`scripts/piano-pdmx-sources.json`, manifest `site/piano-pdmx.js`). IMSLP was checked first but only offers scans for these works. Each `original.mxl` is bundled unchanged; the reference PDF is engraved from it, not a scan. A Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 transcription was rejected because its layout and hidden text did not engrave cleanly.

`python3 scripts/build-musicxml-library.py scripts/piano-pdmx-sources.json site/piano-pdmx.js` engraves with Verovio (pip install verovio; Playwright Chromium and Poppler for the reference PDF). Timing comes from the MusicXML durations, which handle cadenza measures; every note keeps its MusicXML id through Verovio so each attack is tied to its printed notehead, and Verovio's pitch is cross-checked against the MusicXML pitch for every note. Notes the transcriber hid for MuseScore playback are left out, tied notes sound once, and hands follow the printed staff. Compared with the MIDI that MuseScore exported for each transcription, 94–99.8% of attacks match within 1/16 beat; the rest are rolled chords, grace notes and trills that MuseScore realizes for playback. When the chosen transcription has no fingerings, the build can copy printed fingerings from another public-domain transcription of the same piece (`fingeringFrom` in the sources file); a fingering is copied only onto a note with the same onset, pitch and hand, and the build refuses a source that lines up for fewer than 85% of its fingerings. Tristesse and Op. 10 No. 4 take theirs from the OpenScore Transcriptions editions and the C♯-minor Waltz from theclarkster's transcription (all CC0); the C♯-minor Nocturne keeps its own transcriber's fingering. Pieces with no free fingering source are shown as written. Then run `scripts/add-pdmx-curriculum.py`. The shared text font for these engravings is `site/verovio-text.css` (Leipzig, SIL Open Font License).

## Rebuilding

The original repertoire remains in `piano-library.js`; additions and original studies are separate manifests loaded into the same player. New source manifests can be passed to `read-piano-library-midi.cjs ROOT SOURCES` and `build-piano-library.py ROOT SOURCES OUTPUT`. This keeps existing library builds unchanged.

`python3 scripts/build-piano-studies.py /path/to/lilypond` regenerates all 114 original studies (20 Level 0 pieces, 24 key studies, 70 first-reading miniatures): LilyPond sources, source PDFs, MIDI, tagged notation and the content-hashed lazy-load manifest `site/piano-studies.js`; the ids are written to `scripts/piano-study-sources.json`. Work files go to `/tmp/journey-studies` (or `$JOURNEY_STUDIES_DIR`). `python3 scripts/build-piano-studies.py --sources-only DIR` only writes `DIR/<id>.ly`, with no LilyPond needed; the tests use it to confirm that the build reproduces every published source. Requires LilyPond 2.24.4, Node, Python and Poppler. The sources declare `\version "2.24.4"`, which the PyPI 2.24 wheels (2.24.3 at most) refuse, so use the official release:

```
curl -L https://gitlab.com/lilypond/lilypond/-/releases/v2.24.4/downloads/lilypond-2.24.4-linux-x86_64.tar.gz | tar -xz -C /tmp
python3 scripts/build-piano-studies.py /tmp/lilypond-2.24.4/bin/lilypond
```

`pip install --break-system-packages lilypond` (2.25.x; binary at `python3 -c "import lilypond; print(lilypond.executable())"`) also compiles the sources, but its engraving can differ slightly from 2.24.4. All original studies are released as CC0 1.0. Individual repertoire README files retain the source license and attribution.

Build checks reject compilation errors, missing attacks in whole-page or moving notation, and incomplete timelines. Tests (`tests/piano-studies.test.cjs`) compare downloaded MIDI with practice data, require complete bars (eight, or 8–16 for Level 0), both hands except the one-hand Level 0 pieces, and no fingering in any source, practice data or engraving; check each Level 0 piece's stage (white keys, single notes and nothing faster than quarters through piece 8, the right positions and hands, F♯, B♭, 3/4, slurs and staccato, open fifths, eighth pairs only in piece 20); check every generated miniature against its level's keys, meters, rhythms, range, left-hand pattern and tonic ending; check the key-study levels; and rebuild every source to confirm it is unchanged. Browser QA checks key spelling, loading, source images, first-reading seen state and the sampled audio controls.
